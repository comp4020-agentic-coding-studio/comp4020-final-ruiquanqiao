# The Road Rash ledger

This game is a remake of the arcade half ("Thrash") of **Road Rash**, EA's 1994
3DO game in its 1996 Windows port — the version Chinese players know as 暴力摩托
and the one I played. Every mechanic of that game has a row here, sourced, with
a decision against it. A mechanic that is not in this table has not been
considered yet, and nothing gets built from memory: a new mechanic is
researched and added here before any code is written for it.

`spec/ledger.test.ts` holds the table to its own rules:

- every row has a status of `built`, `planned` or `omitted`
- a `planned` row names the crit it is due by: `C8`, `C9`, `C10` or `final`
- a `built` row names the spec test that proves it, as `file › test name`, and
  that test exists
- an `omitted` row says why
- every source tag resolves to a URL in [Sources](#sources)

**Treatment** is `same` when the row is copied as the original has it, or
`adapted:` followed by what changes and why. Where versions of the game disagree,
the PC version wins; the others are only used where the PC manual is silent,
and the row says so.

A row whose source is `—` is a rule the original never needed — matchmaking, for
one — and must be `adapted:`, saying what it answers instead.

The research behind this table is in
[road-rash-research.md](road-rash-research.md), gathered on 30 Sep 2026 from the
manuals and FAQs listed at the end. It has the numbers, the differences between
versions and the conflicts between sources that the table only summarises.

| N6 | Upper-left indicator of every human's position, scrollable | PCM | planned | same | C9 |
| N7 | Chat: canned messages on F1–F8, free typing on F9; status events (busted, wrecked, quit) posted to it | PCM | planned | adapted: canned taunts and the status events only. Free text on an open public site would need moderation this game has no one to do | C9 |
| N8 | Every other rider is drawn where they are now and moving smoothly, however unevenly the network delivers them | — | built | adapted: the original raced on a LAN. Here snapshots are placed by the race time they carry, drawn by a clock that is eased and never stepped back, and carried forward to the present; measured against the live server's own arrival times, every bike is drawn within 0.11 m of how it really moved, frame after frame. How far in the past the others are interpolated follows how late snapshots are coming (N14): 0.1 s on a connection that delivers on time, longer as the 95th percentile of the last five seconds' lateness grows, up to 0.4 s; it rises at a quarter of real time and falls at a fiftieth. | N9 | A match is accepted before it starts | — | built | adapted: added by me, after the ready check of a modern matchmade game; the original's host simply started. When a pool fires, everyone in the room gets a full-screen Match Found with ten seconds to accept, and sees a box fill as each person does. All accept and it loads. Anyone who declines or lets the ten seconds run out leaves the queue; everyone else goes back to the front of the pool with their votes kept. It asks even one person alone in an NPC race. While still in the queue a bar across the top of the lobby shows the pool, a running clock and when the room forms, on a timer so it keeps counting in a background tab | `spec/lobby.test.ts › asks everyone in a room to accept, and starts loading only once all have` |
| N10 | Everyone starts the race together | — | built | adapted: added by me, since the original raced on a LAN. After the accept, everyone loads the race — road, scene, the riders' frames, the music — on a screen with one card per person showing their leathers, their ping and how far they are. The race starts when all are loaded, after at least 2 s so the screen is seen and at most 20 s so one slow browser cannot hold the room. Each person's start carries the countdown shortened by half their round trip, the shortest one seen while loading as NTP takes it: averaged, a page busy building its scene read 147 ms on the same machine. Two browsers showed GO 1 ms apart | `spec/lobby.test.ts › shortens each person's countdown by half their round trip, so GO lands at the same moment for everyone` |
| N11 | Races in progress can be found and watched | — | built | adapted: added by me; the original had no spectators. A Live page at /live lists every race on the road, once a second: road, level, mode, race clock, each person with their leathers (and whether they have left), how many NPCs and how many people are watching. Watch puts the race full screen behind any rider, ← → (or A D, or the arrows on screen) moves the camera between the people and the leading NPC with that rider's own dash, and Esc goes back to the list. Watching survives joining and leaving a queue; racing or being matched ends it. The small view of the newest race for people waiting in a queue stays (N5) | `spec/lobby.test.ts › lists every race on the road with the people in it, and lets anyone watch one, and stop` |
| N12 | A standing across races | — | built | adapted: added by me; the original kept a career for one player. A Leaderboard page at /leaderboard ranks every person who has raced: a finish scores by its place across the line, F1's 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1, with NPCs counted in the place but never listed; wrecked, busted, leaving or running out of time scores nothing. Ties go to wins, then podiums, then fewer races. It is worked out from the results already kept for every race, so races run before it existed count too, and one's own row is marked. A rider made by a script (a network probe, the load test, the spec's two people) connects with `?probe=1`, is marked for good, and is never listed; the riders scripts made before the mark existed were marked by hand where the database shows it: made in bursts seconds apart, or by the probes of 13–14 Oct | `spec/persistence.test.ts › ranks everyone who has raced by points for their finishes, wins and podiums, and lists no NPC (N12)` |
| N13 | Each player's rider is told apart from the others | — | built | adapted: added by me, since the original's network riders were dealt a look by slot. Leathers are dealt when the room forms, a shuffle of eight pairs, one each, so no two people in a room look alike and everyone's entry list carries the same pair. A picker came first, and two people could pick the same; it was taken out. Black & acid and Orange & red were cut after all ten were worn by the rider on the road: the black legs and bike vanished into the asphalt, and the two warm colours smeared into one | `spec/lobby.test.ts › are dealt by the room, never the same twice among its people, and the same in everyone's entry list` |
| N14 | Everyone's connection is watched through the race, and shown | — | built | adapted: added by me; the original raced on a LAN and showed no ping. The round trip was measured only while loading (N10), its shortest kept to time GO, so a connection that went bad in the second minute was invisible, and the only other measurement was `scripts/netprobe.ts` from a test machine. Now the server asks everyone racing for a round trip every second and keeps all of them; each browser reports every five seconds how long a key took to come back acknowledged, the gaps between snapshots (past 250 ms is a stall), how often its own bike was put back where the server said, and how long its frames took; the server times its own ticks and steps. One `net` line per person when they finish or leave, a `lag_spike` line when a round trip runs past 150 ms and three times their usual or snapshots stall, at most one per ten seconds each, and a `server_stall` past 100 ms late. The last round trip sits in the race's top right corner, green under 80 ms, amber under 150, red past it, and each person's usual one is on the Live page. `readlog.ts --ev net,lag_spike,server_stall,server` reads it back | `spec/net.test.ts › writes one line per person when the race ends, by name, with their round trip, jitter and what their browser saw` |
